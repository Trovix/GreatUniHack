# Earshot

**Your lecture, in your ears.**

## Short description

Earshot helps blind students follow university lectures by bringing visual context into the spoken explanation. It brings together a lecture transcript and matching Moodle notes, explains visual references in spoken language, and produces narration with synchronized text, playback controls, source links, and downloadable audio.

## About the project

A lecture recording can preserve every word and still lose part of the explanation. “This curve,” “the table on the left,” and “as you can see” all depend on a picture the listener may not have in front of them.

Earshot connects the recording’s transcript with its lecture notes and slides, then creates a spoken explanation that carries that context with it. A graph becomes a description of its axes and relationships. The listener gets an audio lesson, a word-synchronized reader, controls for speed and seeking, and links back to the course material.

The current demo follows a microeconomics opening lesson about decisions under risk. Its narration describes a graph using wealth on the horizontal axis and utility on the vertical axis.

## The story

“As you can see.”

For a blind student listening to a lecture, those four words can mark the moment an explanation becomes inaccessible. The lecturer points to a curve, compares two columns, or gestures towards an equation. A recording preserves the words, but the meaning carried by the slide stays out of reach.

Earshot starts at that moment.

Ask for your latest lecture. Earshot brings together the Moodle transcript and its matching notes, then works through the references that depend on seeing a slide. The important work is recovering the meaning carried by the visual and making that meaning audible. Before discussing a graph, for example, the narration can establish what each axis represents.

The result is a lesson you can listen to, with text that follows each spoken word. You can slow down, speed up, pause, or jump to another point without losing your place. The full explanation is available to read, the original course sources remain linked, and the audio can be saved for later.

We built Earshot around blind students because access to the words should also mean access to the explanation. The same approach helps anyone revising by listening, whether on a walk or during a commute. The explanation should travel with the student.

Earshot brings the lecture out of the slides and into the listener’s day.

## One-sentence pitch

Earshot pairs lecture transcripts with their slides and notes to make the missing visual context understandable through audio.
